## Claims

1. [t=1:04–2:29] Foil anatomy (5 layers): clear mylar carrier → glue/size layer →
   pigment layer (colored, vaporized aluminium — NOT real gold/silver) → second
   glue/size layer that bonds to the substrate. The substrate-side adhesive is
   formulated per material, which is WHY one foil can't be used on everything.
2. [t=2:29–3:36] Foil–substrate matching is supplier-driven: tell the supplier the
   substrate (paper, board, wood, leather, plastic) and get the correct formulation.
   Wrong foil = patchy, blotchy, or weak coverage.
3. [t=3:36–4:38] Supply chain: foil is made as jumbo/master rolls, slit down; UK
   standard roll ≈ 25 in / ~600 mm wide, standard length ≈ 122 m (400 ft), with 305 m
   rolls for semi-/automatic machines.
4. [t=4:18–5:40] Width economy rule: order foil ~1–2 cm wider than the printing plate
   (9 cm plate → 10–11 cm foil). "Manufacturers make their money on the foil you
   waste" — but don't buy a unique width per job; small/medium/large tiers suffice.
5. [t=5:40–6:20] Cost order-of-magnitude: business-card-size impression (85×55 mm)
   ≈ ¼–⅓ p per impression → ~£3–5 of foil for 1,000 cards (2021). Foil looks
   precious but is cheap at small format.
6. [t=7:24–8:07] Color system: metallics, matte/gloss, holographics exist — but
   foils DO NOT comply to Pantone. Expect ~half a dozen candidate blues/greens/reds
   and pick the closest; an exact Pantone match is unlikely.
7. [t=8:07–8:49] Usage distribution: gold, silver, and rose gold ≈ 90% of all foil
   printing; black/white/matte/gloss/holographic make up the remaining ~10%.
8. [t=8:49–9:32] Shade depth within one "color": Kurtz/Foilco charts show many golds
   (light, dark, greenish, red-gold, bronze, matte, satin) — specify by chart, and
   don't overwhelm clients with shade menus [t=9:32–9:53].
9. [t=9:53–10:53] Code chaos: every foil company uses its own incompatible code
   scheme (numbers, letters, mixes). Record the exact supplier + code per job for
   reorder traceability.
10. [t=10:53–11:54] Foil ≠ foil: toner/sublimation foils and heat-transfer film
    ("ribbon" foils, smaller rolls / thinner cores) are different products that
    cannot run on standard hot-foil machines — and vice versa.
