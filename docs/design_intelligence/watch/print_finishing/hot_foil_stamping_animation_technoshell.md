## Claims

1. [frames fa_02–fa_04] Machine anatomy of stroke hot-foil stamping on a 3D part:
   foil web runs horizontally between two spools across the press bed; above it a
   HEATED SILICONE DIE on a vertical ram; below, the part (a black cosmetic jar lid)
   sits in a HOLDING FIXTURE. Labeled stack (fa_04): Heated Silicone Die → Foil →

2. [frames fa_04–fa_06] Process: die presses down through the foil web onto the
   held part; heat + pressure transfer the pigment layer; die retracts leaving gold
   ornament stamped on the lid (fa_05–fa_06).
3. [frame fa_07] Spent-web evidence: after the stroke, the foil web shows a
   negative "ghost" of the stamped artwork — foil is consumed per impression as an
   area of web, corroborating the Hot Foil Guru's width/waste economics.
4. [frames fa_03–fa_06] Direct decoration of a curved/3D plastic part (jar lid) —
   hot foil is not paper-only; silicone die conforms to the part surface.
5. [frame fa_08] Demonstrated output register: fine ornamental filigree lace-work
   in gold foil on a disc — intricate scrollwork survives the foil process at small
   scale.
