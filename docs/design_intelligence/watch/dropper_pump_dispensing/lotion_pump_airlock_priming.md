## Claims

1. `t=1:11` The spring's one purpose is stroke return — the pump head comes back up
   so it can be used again.
2. `t=1:19`–`t=1:44` Actuation chain: actuator (the part pressed) → rod moves down →
   a collar on the rod engages the piston → piston moves down with the rod.
3. `t=1:53`–`t=2:41` Compression phase: piston descent shrinks the chamber volume,
   raising air/product pressure inside the chamber.
4. `t=2:41`–`t=2:49` If the rod moved without carrying the piston, chamber volume
   would barely change — the rod-to-piston engagement is what does the work.
5. `t=3:35` Pumps are designed sealed ("not to leak"), which is also why they are
   hard to disassemble by hand.
6. `t=3:43`–`t=4:01` A ball sits at the bottom of the chamber, usually inside the
   spring's end: it seals the dip-tube opening against backflow, and its smooth
   shape lets it lift out of the way when liquid is drawn up.
7. `t=4:24`–`t=4:48` Outlet valving is positional: holes at the end of the rod are
   sealed by the piston at rest; they are exposed only after the rod has moved down
   to touch the piston — only then can chamber contents escape up the rod.
8. `t=4:56`–`t=5:20` Discharge drops chamber pressure; liquid rises up the dip tube
   like a straw — suction is the pressure differential, not a moving part.
