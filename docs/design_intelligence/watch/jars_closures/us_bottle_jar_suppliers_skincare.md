## Claims

1. [t=0:00–0:42] Problem framed: Alibaba/AliExpress force large MOQs (500–1000
   units [t=2:27]) with long shipping; US suppliers solve speed + quantity for
   small brands.
2. [t=0:42–1:44] Supplier 1 — SKS Bottle & Packaging: glass + plastic, many
   jar/bottle geometries, and jar+lid sold TOGETHER (no separate
   lid-compatibility work); searchable by ounce. Case quantities ~24–25
   [t=2:06] — small enough to test.
3. [t=3:49–4:31] Suppliers 2–4 (all US): Containers & Packaging
   (containers.packaging.com), FH Packaging, and packaging-supplies-direct
   (ex-Treco/Bruin, recently renamed) — these sell jars and lids SEPARATELY.
4. [t=4:31–5:35] The neck-dimension discipline: on Containers & Packaging each
   jar page lists compatible lids by exact neck dimension — understanding neck
   dimensions is what prevents buying mismatched lids (speaker nearly did).
5. [t=5:55–6:15] Cross-supplier compatibility: the US suppliers largely carry
   the SAME products — if one lacks a lid, another likely stocks it (shared
   standard components, interchangeable by neck spec).
6. [t=6:15–6:57] Sampling economics: Containers & Packaging samples are free
   (pay shipping only); others charge cents per sample — sampling before
   volume purchase is presented as non-negotiable, especially at launch/rebrand
   [t=8:18–8:39].
7. [t=2:47–3:28] Over-buying trap (lived experience): bought too many jars,
   now locked into an old look until stock depletes — small quantities preserve
   the ability to rebrand.
8. [t=8:59–10:03] Glass caveat as business math: glass = heavier = higher
   shipping cost per unit; use glass surgically (2 oz dropper bottles for
   serums/oils where weight is negligible) rather than large glass jars.
