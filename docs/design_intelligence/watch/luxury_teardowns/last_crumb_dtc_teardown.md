## Claims

1. [t=0:43–1:03] Reading construction from artifact: flute spacing identifies
   E- vs B-flute; digital printing can crush corrugate slightly ("feels thin") —
   the print process leaves physical evidence.
2. [t=1:03–1:43] Tear strip as experience: full-width tear strip that opens
   cleanly WITHOUT ripping halfway is the quality test; the opened flap carries a
   copy moment ("born in LA, members-only cookies") — the opening action itself
   is a branded beat.
3. [t=2:04–2:24] Oversize dielines: when a box exceeds one sheet in print or
   converting, the dieline is CROSS-CUT into pieces joined by glue flaps —
   size-driven segmentation, not a defect.
4. [t=3:32–4:12] One-color economy device: on a single-color box, one large foil
   stamp (inside lid here) adds light movement and a custom feel — foil as the
   cheap route to premium on minimal-ink builds (corroborates Being's
   "restraint + one deboss" and the Hot Foil Guru's small-area cost claims).
5. [t=4:53–5:56] Insert waste discipline: the divider insert should be nested
   into the waste areas of the parent box dieline so platforms/strips come off
   scrap — reduces material per box.
6. [t=6:17–7:20] Insert engineering rules: accordion-fold platforms that PRESENT
   the product tilted toward the opener; account for lift/movement in shipping;
   and keep the dieline SIMPLE because pack-out time is a real cost — if
   assembly is slow, the insert is wrong ("caterpillar" fold-in assembly praised).
7. [t=5:14–5:35] Presentation-first insert logic: stands angle each cookie toward
   the user on opening — presentation must match product hype (same
   unveil-toward-you grammar as the Bulgari teardown).
8. [t=4:32] SKU storytelling: numbered, named products (booklet included) turn a
   dozen-pack into a collection — naming as unboxing content.
