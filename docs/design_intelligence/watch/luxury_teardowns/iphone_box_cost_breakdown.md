## Claims

1. [t=1:51–2:13] Outer construction: laminated turned-edge fiberboard (rigid
   two-piece), offset printed, embossed on logos AND on the printed phone image.
2. [t=2:13–2:53] The sharp-edge secret: 90° edges come from a V-CUT; standard
   paper-over-board boxes use a straight cut → slightly tapered edge. V-cut =
   the visible "Apple crispness" (corroborates the Being note's V-groove vs
   crosscut cost/quality dial — here on the shell, not the insert).
3. [t=2:53] Edge finishing: wrap paper extends to the inner base, and a separate
   board is glued inside to hide all paper edges — no visible raw edges.
4. [t=3:14–3:35] Material shift: ~95% of current iPhone packaging is molded pulp
   (replaced plastic trays); only remaining plastic is the PET screen film.
5. [t=4:37–5:38] Pulp grades: egg-carton pulp = COLD press (frayed, porous);
   Apple-grade near-plastic precision pulp = WET press — the process name to ask
   suppliers for; high-grade pulp is hard to source but not volume-gated.
6. [t=3:55–4:15] Cheat exposed: brands commonly pair a thick outer shell with a
   weak inner box to save money — the quality read collapses on opening.
7. [t=4:15, 6:39–7:20] Cost anatomy (at ~50k units [t=9:25]): rigid box top+bottom
   ≈ $0.85; wet-press pulp tray ≈ $0.30; screen cover $0.03; wall-plug cover
   $0.02; headphone wrap $0.04; cable wrap $0.03 → total ≈ $1.27 per unit.
8. [t=9:25–9:46] Volume knee: pricing efficiencies largely arrive around the
   ~50,000-unit range — most machine/print optimization is captured there, well
   below Apple-scale volumes.
9. [t=7:41–8:22] Frustration-free opening rule: every element opens by a single
   pull-and-release (die-cut tear paper wraps, no zip ties, no scissors) —
   opening ergonomics engineered across every component.

## Engine hooks
