## Claims

1. [t=0:20–1:05] Outer-shipper reality: even Bulgari uses a cheap standard RSC
   shipper; first touchpoint inside was a foil-stamped envelope (return form +
   thank-you card) — the envelope, not the box, carries the first brand moment.
2. [t=1:05–1:47] Unboxing action grammar: the consumer should UNVEIL TOWARD
   themselves — never push contents away, never dump them out. Tight fit +
   unbranded inner box forced a dump-out here (product lands upside down) = a
   design failure regardless of material quality.
3. [t=1:47–2:50] Closure tuning: metal tabs + magnets with too-high attraction
   made opening a struggle — magnet strength must be selected for a SEAMLESS
   opening, not maximum hold (counterpoint to the Being note's "3 magnets so it
   doesn't pop": the spec is a window, not a maximum).
4. [t=4:10–4:33] Detail vocabulary observed: faux wax seal (rubber) hiding the
   ribbon end; laser-cut textured panel; hot-stamped logos; specialty (not
   custom) paper in two slightly different colors [t=4:33–4:53]; logo DEBOSSED
   into interior fabric [t=4:53–5:13].
5. [t=5:33–6:53] Emotional-arc rule (the core teardown finding): the sequence ran
   luxury→generic→luxury (branded envelope → blank white box → gorgeous magnetic
   box) — anticipation must BUILD monotonically to the product reveal; a generic
   step mid-sequence resets the emotion to zero.
6. [t=3:50–4:10, 7:13] Multi-sense audit: scent (fragrance samples perfuming the
   whole box) praised as sensory branding; loud crinkly tissue criticized —
   sound is a spec: softer tissue exists and reads more luxurious.
7. [t=7:13–7:56] Vellum as final veil: a translucent layer that lets the logo
   ghost through = one controlled layer before the reveal — recommended over a
   pile of crumpled tissue (insert + vellum instead of tissue mass).
8. [t=8:37] Universal furniture: recycling instructions belong on ALL packaging,
   luxury included.
