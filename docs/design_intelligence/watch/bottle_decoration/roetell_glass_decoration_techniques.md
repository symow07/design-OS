## Claims

1. [t=0:23–1:07] Decal (transfer) decoration: water-applied decals hand-positioned
   on the bottle, squeegeed to purge water/air (no wrinkles, unbroken pattern),
   then baked in a ~10 m oven to set.
2. [t=1:07–1:28] Bake-temperature window is the color-accuracy control: too low →
   glaze doesn't melt, dull color; too high → bottle deforms, pattern scorches.
   Fired decoration lives inside a narrow thermal window.
3. [t=1:28–1:51] Screen printing on glass: ceramic paint applied while the bottle
   ROTATES (rotary screen), then furnace-fixed; multi-color designs traditionally
   need one operation per color.
4. [t=1:51–2:14] Automatic rotary screen machines can wrap a design 360° around
   the bottle and lay 4 colors in one take — automation lifts the color-per-pass
   ceiling.
5. [t=2:14–2:54] Spray coating: colored powder sprayed for all-over color (e.g.
   metallic finishes); quality depends on the powder-coating equipment — "ask the
   manufacturer if their equipment is the most advanced" (self-serving but
   directionally the right supplier question).
6. [t=2:54–3:16] Frosting: bottle soaked in a glaze-powder/acid solvent bath
   ("acid operation") → clear glass becomes frosted; total OR partial frosting
   available.

## Engine hooks

- Claims 1–6 → decoration options matrix for glass bottles: decal-fire / rotary
  screen / spray coat / acid frost — four distinct processes with different
  coverage (motif vs 360° vs all-over vs surface effect), directly usable as the
  option axis in bottle concept specs and supplier RFQs.
- Claim 4 → constraint update: 360° wraparound artwork is manufacturable in one
  pass on automated lines — a continuous-surface analogue of the Zested
  cross-panel continuous-image device, for glass.
- Claim 6 → partial frosting = a masking-based finish → candidate luxury device
  (frost field + clear window) consistent with BBROSE's restraint-outside register.
