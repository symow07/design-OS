## Claims

1. [t=1:02–2:04] Discovery-first: extract brand, audience, competitors, unique
   qualities, packaging type + dimensions, and single-product vs product-line BEFORE
   design; summarize into a creative brief.
2. [t=1:43–2:04] Regulatory gate: packaging content is regulated per country —
   requirements must come from the client's market, not assumed.
3. [t=3:30–3:51] Document setup: print template, high raster quality, CMYK mode.
4. [t=3:51–4:33] The three dieline zones: TRIM line (cut), BLEED (background must
   extend past trim to survive cut drift), SAFE zone (all text/logo/icons inside it).
5. [t=6:18–6:39] Layer discipline: separate locked dieline layer, design layer,
   background layer; background fill built by copying the bleed outline in place.
6. [t=7:00–7:21] Layout method: write out ALL required information, rank by priority,
   then design the hierarchy to guide the consumer through it in order.
7. [t=9:12] Fold-awareness: panels must be designed in their folded orientation —
   example logo rotated 180° to read correctly on the assembled box (independent
   confirmation of the PackMojo top-panel inversion claim).
8. [t=9:47–10:12] Product-line variants: duplicate the master design and use
   Illustrator's Recolor Artwork to derive the rest of the line — structure constant,
   palette as the variant axis.
9. [t=5:34–6:18] Tooling: parametric dieline platforms (Pacdora, 2000+ templates)
   allow dimension edits with live 3D fold preview; the fold/collapse animation is
   named as a core skill-builder for reading dielines.
10. [t=14:21–15:24] Prepress checklist: spellcheck → outline all text (or package
    fonts) → embed all images → deliver AI or PDF at High Quality Print preset.
