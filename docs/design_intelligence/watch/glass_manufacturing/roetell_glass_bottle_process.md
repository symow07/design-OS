## Claims

1. [t=0:01–0:43] Steps 1–2: raw materials = sand, soda ash, limestone, cullet
   (recycled glass), quality-checked incoming; batching portions must be
   accurate for batch-to-batch consistency.
2. [t=0:43–1:25] Step 3 melting: ~1600° furnace produces the molten "gob";
   furnaces run 24/7 for temperature stability and energy efficiency —
   why glass suppliers are volume/schedule-driven businesses.
3. [t=1:25–2:07] Step 4a BLOW-AND-BLOW: for narrow-neck bottles (beer, wine,
   soda) — compressed air blows the gob into a parison, which is flipped
   upright and blown again to final shape.
4. [t=2:28–2:50] Step 4b PRESS-AND-BLOW: for wide-mouth containers (jars,
   candle jars, milk bottles) — a PLUNGER presses the gob into the parison,
   then it's transferred to a blow mold, reheated, and blown.
5. [t=3:17–3:38] Step 5 annealing: slow reheat then gradual cool below the
   strain point in an annealing lehr — stress relief.
6. [t=3:38–4:22] Step 6 QC defect vocabulary (finish-area checks): split
   finish, crumbles, deformation, molded seam, out-of-vertical finish,
   hammered appearance, thin walls, blisters, cord.
7. [t=4:22–4:42] Step 7: surface treatments applied to the exterior AFTER
   forming — the hook where wave-2's decoration matrix (spray, frost, decal)
   attaches to the manufacturing chain.
