## Claims

1. [t=0:41–1:03] First-impression window: shoppers form a judgment of a
   package in ~7 seconds — the design must communicate in a glance.
2. [t=1:23–1:44] Color carries a large share of the buying assessment;
   32% of luxury shoppers say they're more likely to show off / share a
   visually appealing package (unboxing as social display).
3. [t=2:04–2:45] Information transparency (clear ingredients, sourcing,
   claims) builds trust, and trust converts to willingness to pay premium.
4. [t=2:45–3:05] Functionality gates perception: easy to open, handle, and
   store — friction in use degrades the brand read regardless of aesthetics.
5. [t=3:05–3:47] Apple case: the box lid's deliberately slow, air-cushioned
   release is engineered anticipation — the opening experience is designed,
   not incidental.
6. [t=4:29–5:10] Color is culturally coded: red reads wealth/luck in Chinese
   contexts vs danger/warning elsewhere — palettes don't transfer across
   markets unexamined.
7. [t=5:30–6:53] Sustainability economics (as cited, Nielsen et al.): Gen Z
   willing to pay ~10% more for sustainable products; 82% ESG-aware; 75% of
   Millennials factor sustainability; 78% prefer green packaging.
8. [t=6:53–7:34] Differentiation levers: unique shapes, materials, and
   colors are the named routes to standing out in a crowded category.
