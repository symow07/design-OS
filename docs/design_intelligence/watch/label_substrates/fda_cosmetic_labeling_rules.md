## Claims

1. [t=0:26–0:46] Framing: cosmetic labels are LEGAL/REGULATORY documents, not just
   marketing surfaces; improper labeling → relabeling costs, marketplace issues,
   delays.
2. [t=1:06] US mandatory element set: product identity statement, net quantity of
   contents, ingredient declaration, responsible-party information, plus
   product-type-dependent elements.
3. [t=1:26] Ingredient declaration rules: accepted nomenclature (INCI-style),
   must reflect formulation; common failures = missing ingredients, wrong names,
   incomplete lists, outdated terminology.
4. [t=1:46] Net quantity statement failure modes: wrong units, wrong formatting,
   wrong PLACEMENT, inaccurate quantity — placement is itself regulated.
5. [t=2:07–2:28] Claims boundary: any diagnose/treat/cure/prevent language (acne
   treatment, collagen stimulation beyond cosmetic effect, healing, altering body
   structure/function) can reclassify the cosmetic as a DRUG — applies to labels
   AND websites/social/ads.
6. [t=2:28–2:48] Jurisdiction trap: products correctly labeled for their home
   country commonly still fail US requirements — compliance is per-market, no
   mutual recognition.
7. [t=2:48–3:09] MoCRA (Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act) adds facility
   registration + product listing obligations on top of labeling.
