## Claims

1. `t=0:17`–`t=0:49` **Litho** (offset lithography) is the route for
   photographic-quality imagery — cereal cartons and food cartons use it to
   showcase appetizing product photos.
2. `t=1:23`–`t=2:03` **Flexo** can print imagery but it comes out "quite
   grainy and pixelated"; his stated rule: with flexo "stick to … simple
   block artwork, text and line work."
3. `t=2:10`–`t=2:50` Counterexamples shown: single-color flexo achieving fine
   pattern work — printed tissue paper with an "extremely good wallpaper
   effect," and good-quality inside-box printing — flexo "is catching up with
   litho quality all the time."
4. `t=3:07`–`t=3:15` His recommendation for e-commerce corrugated: "flexo
   printing … every time."
5. `t=3:15`–`t=3:37` Litho on corrugated is really **litho-lamination**: print
   litho onto ~**800 micron** cotton board (folding-carton-thickness stock),
   then laminate that sheet onto the corrugate — "a jump of more than forty
   percent in cost."

## Engine hooks

- Claim 5 is the mechanism claim of the wave: you cannot run corrugate through
  a litho press — photographic print on a shipper = a laminated second
  substrate at **~+40% cost**. Engine rule: shipper artwork defaults to
  flexo-safe (solids, line work, ≤2 colors); litho-lam is a premium flag with
  an explicit cost delta.
- Claims 2–4 give the corrugated print-vocabulary constraint: the M7 shipper
