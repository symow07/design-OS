## Claims

1. `t=0:14`–`t=0:30` Flexo = **relief printing**: ink applied to a RAISED
   image on a flexible plate, impressed onto the substrate; growth drivers
   named as print-quality improvements, multi-substrate capability, and
   **water-based inks** (environmental angle).
2. `t=0:39`–`t=0:55` Plate making: expose a **photopolymer plate to UV
   light** — image areas build up and end raised above the plate base.
3. `t=1:02`–`t=1:10` One color = one plate = one plate cylinder = one press
   station; this house prints **up to 10 colors in one pass**.
4. `t=1:19`–`t=1:41` Station anatomy — THREE rolls: **anilox roll** (surface
   of tiny cells carrying ink from reservoir to plate), **plate cylinder**
   (carries the photopolymer plate), **impression cylinder** (supports the
   substrate during transfer).
5. `t=2:07`–`t=2:16` Color QC: automated ink blending; **pre- and post-run
   drawdowns** against approved color standards for run-to-run repeatability.
6. `t=2:24`–`t=2:32` Web economics: print at maximized web width, then SLIT
   to the width the packing equipment needs.
7. `t=2:32`–`t=2:49` **Reverse printing**: mirror-image printed on the film
   backside, then laminated to a base material — ink is buried between film
   layers, protected from scuffing and from product contact.
8. `t=3:04` Modern flexo capability claim: high-def process images, extremely
   small type, difficult vignettes.

## Engine hooks

- Claim 4 vs the offset chain (expresscards note): flexo has NO blanket —
  anilox → plate → substrate. The anilox cell volume is why flexo solids are
  strong but fine gradients historically suffered (Lil Packaging's "grainy"
  claim now has its mechanism). The two press anatomies are the mechanical
  base of the engine's carton-vs-shipper print split.
- Claim 3 generalizes the plates-are-stations rule across BOTH processes:
  every declared ink = a station, offset or flexo. The preflight
  spot-swatch-equals-plate rule is process-independent — engine can state it
  unconditionally.
- Claim 7 is directly relevant to M7 pouches/sachets: reverse-printed
  lamination is why pouch artwork is often mirrored in production files —
  the pouch emitter should carry an `artwork_side: surface|reverse` parameter
  and never assume surface print for laminated film.
- Claim 5 gives the physical color-approval artifact for labels/film:
  drawdowns against a standard — pairs with the owner-review guidance from
  Ryonet (approve physical samples, adjacent-PMS fallback).
- Claim 8 tempers Lil Packaging's 2020 "stick to line work": a capable flexo
  house claims process imagery today. Engine should treat flexo image quality
  as SUPPLIER-DEPENDENT, not process-forbidden — ask the converter, don't
  hardcode.
