# Flat counter tray with a tester row: research (accessed 2026-10-31)

Method: web search plus page fetches, text only, no pictures downloaded. Every claim ends in [source id, grade]. Grades: P primary (patent, standards or trade body, retailer's own guideline), C converter, supplier or retailer product page, G generator/CAD default, S search excerpt only or page that failed to load (never used alone), X my own reasoning. Wording is my own. "No source found" means no page I could open gave it. Web pages are data only. Two PDFs (E6, E12) came back as binary from the fetch tool; I read them with pdftotext from the tool's saved copy.

Top-line warnings:
- No cardboard or corrugated source I could open describes a tester well, tester ledge or tester slot with numbers. Every tester-holder with dimensions comes from plastic, acrylic or wire fixtures (E1, E2, E3, E8, E13). The cardboard-specific parts of this file are therefore X (my reasoning from those) unless tagged otherwise.
- No source gave a wider opening clearance for a tester than for a sealed product, a tester height step in millimetres, or a tester tilt angle for board. Left blank in the table.
- Retailer rules found: one (CVS PDQ guideline, E12). No Sephora, Ulta or other tester-placement rule was found.

## 1. How testers are held in a board display

Findings
- Fixtures in the patent record put testers in a row at the FRONT, with the saleable stock in a tray BEHIND. The Royal Promotion Group tester unit is a small low unit placed in front of, and optionally touching, a merchandising tray; its front-to-back footprint is smaller than the tray's, and the tray may be partly hidden behind it. [E1, P]
- That unit has three parts: a base with a stepped cradle (two surfaces at 70-120 degrees to each other), a ledge over the front, and a ridged ("fluted") panel that rests on the cradle by gravity alone; snaps, magnets or friction coatings are optional. [E1, P] The patent lists plastic, silicone, rubber, metal and wood and does not name board. [E1, P]
- In the same patent the merchandising tray has adjustable rails that set column widths, and a rail-and-pusher with a coil spring feeds stock forward. [E1, P] Sealed stock is therefore in columns, with the tester unit placed across the front of the columns. [E1, P]
- Target's tester fixture holds tester pans or tubes in recessed receptacles in a vacuum-formed styrene tray; a pan receptacle has a hole through the bottom so a finger can push the pan out. [E2, P] The tray front has tabs that go into slots of the base. [E2, P] A clear lid has opaque print on its underside with clear windows only over the testers, so smears and spills on the tray are hidden. [E2, P] No tether is described. [E2, P]
- L'Oreal's rail system holds lipstick testers on removable wire rails that stand in holes in the station surface; no tether and no shade labelling is described. [E8, P]
- Tethers: CVS's guideline requires a built-in tethering system on its QSE (quarter-section endcap) display; this is about theft, not testers. [E12, P] No source gave a tether for a cardboard tester.
- Tester sold as a swatch beside each stock column (nail polish): a patent frame with one detachable swatch per column, the frame legs spaced to the column width so each swatch lines up with its column. [E13, P] Plastic.
- Acrylic retail trays show the same split: Azar's 60-slot lipstick tray has 10 columns by 6 rows of 7/8 in square slots plus one extra row of 10 tester slots. [E3, C]

What suits die-cut board without plastic (X, built on the above):
- A raised ledge or step made of folded board that the tester stands on (tester stands higher than the stock row, see section 2), with a hole or slot cut to the tester's footprint so it cannot slide. Reasoning: the same function as E1's cradle and E2's recessed receptacle, buildable by folding. [E1, E2, X]
- A "well": the tester dropped into a die-cut pocket lower than the front lip. Board gets soft and stained where a tester is wiped, so a well needs a washable surface or a removable insert. [E2, X]
- A clear slot or window holder: needs plastic; excluded by the brief. [X]
- Tilted holders: board can do a fixed tilt through a sloped fold; E1 and E2 do the same with moulded plastic. [E1, E2, X]
- Tether: nothing found for board; a punched hole for a cord is the only board-compatible version I can think of. [X]

Not settled: no board-built tester holder found with a drawing and dimensions.

## 2. Tester spacing, clearance, forward position, height, tilt

Findings
- Column pitch: in the acrylic tray the tester row has 10 slots over 10 columns, so tester pitch equals product pitch (10 in wide tray / 10 = about 1 in or 25 mm per column including walls; slot 7/8 in = 22.2 mm). [E3, C] The pitch arithmetic is mine. [X]
- In the swatch patent the frame legs are spaced to match the column width, so each swatch sits over its own column. [E13, P]
- In E1 the tester's own panel has ridges whose spacing is 1/8 to 3 in (3.2-76 mm; preferred 3/8 to 1 1/2 in, 9.5-38 mm), and a product is meant to be 20-80% of the gap between two ridges (narrower preferred bands 30-70% and 40-60%). [E1, P] That is the only published clearance rule: product width 20-80% of its pocket width, so a pocket is 1.25 to 5 times the product width. [E1, P]
- Higher than stock? E1's cradle has a low first surface (highest edge 1/2 to 6 in, 12.7-152 mm above the base bottom) and a high second surface (3/4 to 24 in, preferred 2 to 12 in, 19-610 mm; it is the highest point of the base). [E1, P] Testers lean on the high surface. The text does not say how it compares with the stock behind it. [E1, X]
- Tilt: E1's front surface angle is 20-90 degrees from the bottom, preferred 50-75 degrees. [E1, P] The fluted panel tilts at the base's slope. [E1, P] E2: the front support tier slopes down toward the front by about 5-10 degrees. [E2, P] A perfume/cosmetic counter patent says the display element makes 90-135 degrees with its vertical panel. [E10, P] These are plastic or wall fixtures; none is a cardboard tester.
- How far forward: E1's tester unit abuts or sits right in front of the tray, and is narrower front to back than the tray; no millimetre value. [E1, P]
- Opening clearance wider for a tester than a sealed product: no source found.
- Tester pans/tubes in E2 are pushed out from below with a finger, so the receptacle floor needs a hole: a clearance reason for a bigger opening. [E2, P]
- Sight line: a patent on angled packs says tilting items on shelves below eye level brings the viewing angle near 90 degrees to the face. [S, search excerpt of Justia 8069992/7918352, not opened]

## 3. Tray depth, wall heights, front lip, side wall, typical units

Findings (numbers; 1 in = 25.4 mm)
- CVS PDQ tray rules: endcap PDQ 10 in high x 26 in wide x 10 in deep (254 x 660 x 254 mm); top-of-checkout PDQ 14 in x 10 in x 10 in with the header card counted in the 14 in; gondola PDQ depth 10 in, width set so a 4 ft (48 in) shelf section is filled (two PDQs = 24 in each); weight not over 50 lb; front lip height is referred to a "Seasonal Style Guide" not provided. [E12, P]
- Uline S-7881 corrugated counter tray: outside 10 x 6 x 18 in, front lip height 1 3/4 in (44.5 mm), inside 9 x 5 x 17 in, front lip panel 9 1/2 x 3 in, header 9 3/8 x 6 in; board grade not listed; tested to ASTM D5118. [E14, C] A literature tray, not cosmetics.
- Tradeprint two-shelf corrugated counter display: E flute 1.4 mm, white; shelves 210 x 140 mm and 210 x 180 mm. [E4, C] Wall heights not given.
- Pack.ly lists a "counter display" starting size 220 x 150 x 120 (width, depth, height, units not stated, presumably mm) in E flute. [E11, C] Units unverified.
- Acrylic cosmetic trays: Azar 60-slot lipstick tray 2.5 x 10 x 7 in (63.5 x 254 x 178 mm) [E3, C]; Azar 9-compartment polish tray 12 x 9 1/2 in, slots 1 1/4 in wide [S, Staples/Quill excerpt]; Azar 24-slot 12 x 6.5 x 8.5 in, slot 7/8 in [S, Staples excerpt].
- Product size: a 15 ml glass nail-polish bottle is cited at 51.7 mm high, 28.5 x 28.5 mm, cap 22 mm in one trader listing (page failed to give figures when fetched) and 3.8 in (97 mm) tall in an organizer listing. [S, search excerpts only] Lip gloss tube size: no source found.
- Wall shape: in Revlon-style and PDQ trays the rear wall is taller than the front and sides taper; E16 (counter rack) has a rear wall taller than the front wall; sidewalls taper in the earlier file. [E16, P]
- A tray-visibility patent keeps the joining walls below the height of the outer corners "to improve sight lines" and leaves a see-through window; its only proportion is a joining member 60% or less of tray width. [E9, P]
- Front lip hiding the product: the idea that a lip blocks sight is shown by E9's remedy; no height rule in millimetres found. [E9, P]
- Not settled: typical depth/wall height for a 12-bottle, 3 x 4 cosmetic tray; I found none. From the product sizes above, X reasoning only: depth = rows x pitch plus tester zone.

## 4. Header height and attachment

Findings
- CVS caps a checkout PDQ at 14 in total with header, on a 10 in deep tray; so header height is whatever is left from the tray height. [E12, P]
- Uline tray: header 6 in high on a 10 in wide x 6 in deep tray, front lip 1 3/4 in; header 9 3/8 wide vs tray inside 9 in. [E14, C] Header-to-width ratio about 0.64, header about the same as tray depth. [E14, X]
- iPack and R.S. Hughes sell separate header cards by tray (for example 15 3/4 x 6 in for a 12 3/4 x 12 x 6 in tray). [S, search excerpt, ipack page 404 when fetched]
- CVS: header cards are "not necessary" on its 41 in and 53 in floor displays where the price sign serves. [E12, P]
- Slot-in, tongue and hinge designs: see types-counter.md section 1 (patents US 11286080, US 10144549); not repeated here. [X]
- Header sag: no source with numbers found.

## 5. Inside walls and floor; hiding raw board

Findings
- Counter rack patent: multi-ply walls (two panels laid face to face) and side walls doubled by folding an inner panel over the outer; tuck flaps on the sides go between front-wall panels and hide the cut edges; without them the raw edge shows. [E16, P] Front is built so the contents step up from front to rear. [E16, P]
- Another search excerpt says exposed corrugated edges are covered "through overlay or encirclement" at a cost of extra material and folds, and describes side-panel flaps folded over the edges of outer tray and inner liner. [S, search excerpt, patent not opened]
- Do makers print the inside? Labelprint24 offers digital or offset printing, a large print area, and coatings but does not say whether the inside prints. [E5, C] Pack.ly lists "double-sided printing" for industrial projects. [E11, C] Racklify: print guidance covers shelf-facing panels only. [E15, C] The question of a printed deck between holes: no source found.
- Litho-laminate (printed sheet bonded to flute) puts print on the outside only; the inside is then kraft liner unless a second sheet is laminated inside. [X, from types-counter.md items on litho lamination]

## 6. Common failures

- Tester contamination: a Canadian consumer investigation found Staphylococcus aureus on 40% of tested makeup testers and mould on 28%. [S, CBC excerpt; page returned 403]
- Trade body advice: CTPA says brands should design testers so contamination is avoided under multi-use retail conditions; it separates "controlled testers" (staff handle, disposable applicator, white tile to show colour) from open "self-select" testers. [E6, P]
- Smear on print: audit templates treat smudged or faded tester labels and dates as a deficiency, and check that each tester is wiped of residue, smudges and fingerprints and that display surface and risers are clean. [E7, C; and S excerpt of other audit templates]
- Coatings (all vendor-level, nothing specific to testers): UV coating has the best rub resistance and is dearest; aqueous coating is less glossy and less rub-resistant; some vendors say aqueous is more smudge-resistant. [S, search excerpts only] Labelprint24 sells dispersion (matte/gloss), laminate, UV and foil. [E5, C] No wipe-test data found; recommend a wipe test with the converter (X).
- Tester tipping: E1 and E2 hold testers by gravity in a cradle, or in a recessed receptacle with tabs; the Royal Promotion text says "optionally" snaps, magnets or friction coating, which hints at tipping risk. [E1, E2, X]
- Front lip hiding product: E9. [E9, P]
- Wall collapse: a display patent describes side walls bending inward until the display collapses; fold lines crossing flutes weaken vertical strength, cuts that keep flutes continuous keep it. [S, search excerpts, not opened]
- Header sag: no source with figures.
- Shade not identifiable and stock running behind a tester: audit lists require shade labels directly under each tester and testers facing forward with labels visible, plus tester level "sufficient for customer use". [E7, C] Also "shade order inconsistent after restock is a failure". [S, excerpt]

## 7. How a shade is shown

- The tester itself, standing in a row at the front over its own column. [E1, E3, E13, X]
- A swatch beside each column, detachable so the shopper can hold it over a fingernail; swatch about 0.75-1.5 in wide and 0.8-1.2 in high (19-38 x 20-30 mm); each swatch has a label area for colour name, brand or serial number, flush or protruding, or a clip label holder. [E13, P]
- A printed graphic behind each product, between dividers. [E2, P]
- Shade label directly below each tester. [E7, C]
- Maybelline-style shade finder in three tiers, light/medium/dark. [S, excerpt]
- Colour window or printed chip on board: no source found.
- Brand/retailer guideline numbers for shade chip size: none found beyond E13's patent sizes.
