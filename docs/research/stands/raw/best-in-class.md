# Best-in-class displays: research (accessed 2026-10-31)

Method: every claim carries [source id, grade]. P = primary (award body, brand, the maker); C = converter or trade press; S = search excerpt only (page not opened, never used alone); X = our synthesis. All wording is ours. Maker statements are labelled "maker claim". No picture was downloaded or sent anywhere. ## What the best do: strength, premium, assembly, material

### Strength
- Reinforce high-stress joints with glue tabs and double-wall risers; where weight sits on a shelf, use locking tabs or cap trays. [D12, C]
- Keep the centre of gravity low on top-heavy loads and test for tipping; give products a loose, not forced, fit and use dividers so the front row does not collapse. [D12, C]
- Use higher flute for deep trays that must resist compression and finer flute on faces that carry print; single wall is typical, double wall for heavy or stacked loads; add internal tabs, glued corners or a chipboard base where rough handling is expected. [D13, C]
- Test compression, vibration, drop and humidity. [D13, C] The makers behind the e.l.f., RRD and Smurfit UK cases state drop, vibration or ISTA testing (maker claims). [D5, P] [D19, P]
- Tabs can bend under load because of the layered board, so tab placement and board choice matter (patent commentary). [D27, S]
- X: strength in the winners came from shape as well as board: closed wraps with integrated back wall (Nivea), a slot-in base panel that locks a two-part body (Revlon), extra printed support parts (1664). [D1, P] [D2, P] [D18, C]

### Premium (structure rather than graphics)
What sources actually say:
- A buyer's brief for premium paper displays named the things to remove: visible fluting, raw edges, clear polyester fill, plastic tape. [D5, P]
- Material mix reads premium: linen, real wood and refined paper (4711); silver-foil lamination (Nivea). [D2, P] [D1, P]
- Smooth coated board takes laminate and high-gloss varnish; solid bleached boards are marketed to cosmetics for that reason (maker claims). [D25, S]
- Matte or satin water-based coating resists scuffs without glare; film laminate only on small hero panels. [D12, C]
- Glued-free plug-in closure with clean die-cut or grooved edges and a transfer finish (Rissmann). [D6, C]
- A wave-shaped pedestal and "organic lines" were what judges named for the Nivea and Visapure units. [D1, P] [D7, P]
- White casing makes a bulky footprint read smaller (Weleda). [D2, P]
X (our reading, not stated as a rule by any source): premium comes from a continuous skin with no visible flute edge, a defined rim, a rigid-feeling body and few visible parts. Sources name the faults to hide but give no recipe for folded rims, double walls, hidden locks, magnets or board weight; those were not found as stated rules.

### Fast assembly
- Ship flat; set up in minutes; no separate manual: colour-coded tabs or printed cues, a printed belly band with steps and a QR video. [D12, C] [D5, P]
- Typical glueless sequence: fold in side tabs, push into slots to lock, slide the header on. (vendor video via search, not opened.) [S excerpt of displays2go, not used alone]
- Scored folds, perforations for controlled opening, tuck-locks reduce assembly and glue. [D13, C]
- Alternatively ship pre-erected or fully pre-packed (Nivea, e.l.f., Colorsonic) which moves labour to the converter. [D1, P] [D4, P] [D8, P]
- Revlon: a single slotted base panel plus a push-on poster; two assembly steps. [D2, P]
- Numbered parts and pictogram sheets cut errors (supplier advice via search, S).

### Saving material and cost
- Design die-lines so trays nest in transit. [D13, C]
- Weleda's white casing over a half pallet and Rissmann's two-piece box (as little material as possible, maker claim) show fewer parts per unit. [D2, P] [D6, C]
- Record a minimisation rationale justifying board weight against function (PPWR practice). [D17, C]
- Standard modular tray heights are offered in three sizes by one converter (252, 288, 347 mm), which saves tooling. [D23, S]

### Sustainable
- Single-material corrugated; avoid plastic windows and mixed laminates. [D13, C]
- Film lamination is the main grade-lowering item; replace with water-based dispersion coatings; limit full-area UV; use alkali-dispersible or water-washable hot-melt and minimal glue; design out non-fibre fittings or make them tool-free to remove; avoid cold foil and dense metallic inks. [D17, C]
- Unit is graded on its weakest separable part. PPWR dates given by the source: below grade C barred from 1 Jan 2030, only A and B from 1 Jan 2038; the source is inconsistent about the 2026 deadline, so check the official text. [D17, C]
- Corrugated inlays replace plastic bubble wrap (Thimm, maker claim); eco-fix replaces polystyrene fixings. [D11, P] [D10, P]
- Lancôme's statement that cardboard trays replaced plastic and removed magnets is a brand claim. [D9, S]
- Glueless: the 1664 source does not mention glue; Rissmann is explicitly glue-free. [D18, C] [D6, C]
- Print recycling instructions on the outer shipper; keep teardown simple. [D12, C]

### Common criticisms and failure modes
- Corrugated is cheap and recyclable but degrades fast and looks less premium than acrylic or metal (retail design guide via search, S, not used alone).
- Shipping damage is the usual failure of flimsy displays. [D12, C]
- Glossy finishes cause glare; UPC and price-sign zones become unreadable after restocking; retailer footprint and header rules ignored; no pilot tests. [D12, C]
- Looks sustainable but is not recyclable (lamination, UV, glue, plastic parts). [D17, C]
- All "results" found (sales lift, interaction) are maker claims with no figures. [D1, P] [D4, P] [D5, P] [D8, P]
