# Other stand types: research (accessed 2026-10-31)

Method: every bullet ends with [source id, grade]. P = primary (patent, standard body, board maker handbook), C = converter/printer/retailer guide, G = generator/CAD default, S = search excerpt only (page not opened; never used alone), X = my synthesis. Wording is our own. "Not found" means no opened source gave the number. Inch figures from CVS are converted to mm by X (1 in = 25.4 mm) and shown beside the original.

Reading note on grades: B1 (CVS) is a retailer's own display specification, graded C because it is a buyer-side guide, not a standards body. No FEFCO/ECMA catalogue itself could be opened (see "Not found"), so no code below is graded P.

---

## 0. Cross-cutting facts (apply to all types)

- Corrugated displays are mono-material, supplied flat, printed flexo or litho, and sold as standard or custom designs; shelf count, header shape, footprint are the usual custom variables [B4, C] [S6, S].
- Smurfit says its FSDUs are "strong enough" but gives no load figures, no board grade names, no sizes, no assembly time [B4, C].
- Flute guidance seen only in excerpts: E-flute is the usual display/fitment flute, B and C carry strength; EB double wall is described as fine to about 32 kg evenly spread [S9, S]; BC double wall is the heavy option [S9, S]. One supplier lists a heavy floor display at 525 x 346 x 1498 mm in BC flute [S5, S]. None of these is a test-backed rule; treat as pointers.
- Board test floors seen only in excerpts: Academy requires corrugated PDQ displays in ECT32 or stronger [S3, S]; Dollar Tree lists ECT 32 lb/in minimum for displays of 20-50 lb with C or BC double wall allowed for the heavier tier [S4, S].
- Flute thickness from a converter page: E about 1.5 mm, B about 3 mm, EB about 4.5 mm [B13, C].
- Corrugation direction matters: a patented stand runs the main sheet corrugations horizontally "relative to gravity" and claims better load strength than vertical [B8, P]. A different patent runs shelf corrugations front-to-back and wall corrugations vertical [B6, P]. These disagree on wall direction; the first is a claim, the second a worked example, neither tested. [X]
- Moisture is the standing enemy: patents cite delamination and collapse from humidity or mopping, and answer with water-resistant starch glue and wax coat [B8, P] [B7, P].
- Retailer weight cap: every CVS off-shelf display type is capped at 50 lb (about 22.7 kg) [B1, C]. A UK co-packer cites manual-handling guidance capping stock in a shelved FSDU at 23 kg [B2, C]. The two agree within rounding. [X]

## 1. Rotating / carousel units

- Purpose: counter or floor spinner so a small footprint offers many faces; seen for lip balm, nail polish, small cards and blister items [S15, S]. Counts: a lip balm spinner excerpt claims about 64 tubes [S15, S]; a 2-tier counter spinner with 12 pegs is sold in plastic [S15, S]. Not cosmetics-corrugated specific.
- Patent-described paperboard counter carousel: wooden stationary base block, steel support rod set into it, a hollow hexagonal rotary shell (six faces, may be more or fewer) cut from one paperboard blank, with scores for the faces, glued flanges inside, top and bottom closures, and an optional inner stiffener [B5, P].
- The bearing is an eyelet with a flange, fixed on the rod at the factory by punch upsetting; the flange carries the shell's bottom so the shell spins; the merchant only drops the shell on [B5, P].
- Preferred board for the load-bearing shell and bottom: double-faced corrugated paperboard, with creased folds [B5, P].
- Product holders: product is stuck to flat cards with double-faced adhesive strips; each face has a pair of lateral restraints and one axial restraint, U-shaped channels glued to the card, that receive a slide-in card; several cards may sit on one face; cap is an inverted V with two flanges tucked into two slots [B5, P].
- Tower-style spinner (mostly plastic, cardboard listed as an option): base with a spinning member inside a fixed member; the tower is a multi-sided tube, example nine sides, built from three identical three-panel sections joined by overlapping flaps; removable trays hook onto slits (vertical slits at borders, horizontal slits along the length); a lazy-Susan ball bearing may sit under a cap [B11, P]. Several towers can share one base, no more than two deep [B11, P].
- Ship flat: the tower is built from flat panel sections joined on site [B11, P]; the carousel shell is glued from a blank, so it ships as a folded or assembled shell [B5, P] (flat state of the shell: not stated).
- Sightlines: product cards face outward on all faces; a counter unit is seen from all sides. Eye height figures: not found.
- Common failures: not stated by sources. Mechanical risk by synthesis: spin axis load falls on one bearing and the board around it, so a bottom reinforcement plate is needed (the patent uses double-faced board and an optional stiffener) [B5, P] [X].
- Dimensions of the shell, rod and base: not found in B5 (no dimensions given); B11 gives none either.

## 2. Shelf-ready / retail-ready packaging (SRP, RRP, PDQ)

- What it is: a shipping case that becomes the shelf unit: tray (lower) plus cover, cover removed along a perforation or by tear tape; some covers are film [B3, C].
- Retailer rule sources named: ECR Europe "Shelf Ready Packaging" and the ECR UK Blue Book (FEFCO calls it the SRP "bible"); Costco structural spec, Target shelf-ready standards, Walmart "RRP and PDQ Display Standard Style Guide" - similar, not identical [B3, C] [S11, S]. The Blue Book itself was not opened.
- ECR principles in excerpts: easy to identify, open, replenish, dispose [S16, S]. Opening must be intuitive; retailers discourage box cutters [B3, C]; Walmart summary says perforations must tear clean by hand or the case is rejected [S3, S].
- Tray-and-hood style: a hood sits over a tray; plain tear-off leaves a rough edge on the tray that stays on the shelf, which a patent sets out to fix [S11, S]. Converters offer one-piece packs that tear open and leave a tray [S11, S].
- Style code: one converter lists its dairy shelf pack as "Fefco: 1000" [S11, S]. This is not an ordinary FEFCO four-digit family, so I cannot call it a standard code; likely a converter's own reference. [X]
- Hinged shelf packaging (unfoldable) exists as a separate product line [S11, S]; details not opened.
- Product count and module sizes (600 x 400 etc.): not found; Wikipedia gives none [B3, C].
- PDQ tray sizes seen in one retailer guide: endcap PDQ 10 in high x 26 in wide x 10 in deep (254 x 660 x 254 mm), gondola PDQ depth 10 in, width such that PDQs per 4 ft shelf total 48 in, top-of-checkout PDQ 14 x 10 x 10 in (356 x 254 x 254 mm) including header [B1, C].
- Cosmetics-specific SRP in the same guide: "SSCU cosmetic prepack" 8 in high (not over 10 in with product) x 23 7/8 in wide x 12 in deep (203 x 606 x 305 mm), product space 17 1/2 x 11 1/4 in, graphic panel 3 3/4 x 11 3/4 in, corrugate with 5/8 in white border on the angled part, wells black, graphics only on the designated panel, logo 100 % black, max 4 1/2 in wide, right-justified 1/2 in from the edge of the front tip-on [B1, C].
- The elevated beauty endcap PDQ: a 2 in tray, 23 3/4 in wide, 10 in deep; four PDQs of product fit within 39 in of shelf height, three adjustable shelves 1 3/4 in thick that cannot be removed [B1, C].
- Glue zones, board grade, tear-line geometry (distance from rim, perforation pitch): not found.

## 3. Hanging units and clip strips

- Purpose: lightweight carded or blister items (lip gloss cards, nail polish cards, small sets) hung from a power wing, shelf front or pegboard [B1, C] [S7, S].
- Clip strip, cardboard-free: a molded polypropylene strip with 8 or 12 clips and an optional header [S7, S]; Kinter-type strip about 22.5 to 24.25 in long, 12 stations, 1 1/8 in wide, 0.050 in thick, header 1 3/8 in high [S7, S]. Weight rating for cosmetics: not found.
- CVS clipstrip envelope: 30 in high x 6 in wide x 1 1/2 in deep (762 x 152 x 38 mm), hung from a power wing dress kit on a supplier-provided S-hook, supplier responsible for strip strength [B1, C].
- CVS miniwing: 45 in x 6 in x 4 in (1143 x 152 x 102 mm), hangs by a U-clip with 1 in inside dimension, slotted 3 in from the top so the top sits flush with the dress kit top [B1, C].
- CVS skinny tower (floor, peg-hook): 59 3/4 in total including a 12 in base, 6 in wide and deep tower on a 10 x 10 in base, plastic peg hooks max 2 in, one-sided, price label space 1 1/4 x 2 in [B1, C].
- Lockers (peg towers sitting on a gondola deck): 51 1/2 in high, 13 in deep, width 11 1/2 in (4 per 4 ft section) or 15 1/2 in (3 per 4 ft); headers 3 in high, footers 6 in [B1, C].
- Card hang hole: a Euro slot (inverted T) is standardised in practice; a card-maker page gives no size; Sprintis cardboard hang tabs are rated up to 350 g; a 50 x 27 mm PET reinforcement is sold for slots that tear [S8, S]. Slot dimensions in mm: not found (a requested 32 x 5 mm figure was not confirmed).
- Typical hole-to-edge distance 10-15 mm is a generic encyclopedia statement, not a retailer spec [S14, S].
- Hook patent on clip strips: card has a hole at the top through which the strip peg passes [S7, S].
- Failures: strip must keep carded weight on; supplier owns the engineering [B1, C]. No other failures found.

## 4. Floor stands (FSDU)

- Purpose: free-standing, off-shelf shelf stack for seasonal, gift-set and launch product; flat-packed and set up in store [B4, C].
- Patent anatomy (knock-down floor display): one rear wall (example 48 x 36 in), two side walls folded back on themselves to double thickness, three shelves hinged to the rear wall by a glued flap, front edge doubled under and stapled; optional reinforcing layers inside the double walls up to about 18 in above the lowest shelf and inside shelves, of higher test board (275 lb) [B6, P].
- Shelf support in that patent: a rope under each shelf, knotted through keyhole slots in the side walls with split washers; shelf side edges also grip the side walls [B6, P].
- Another patent: single corrugated sheet forms rear wall and two side walls; shelves are folded corrugated with a reinforcing sheet between layers and fold up flat against the back; coated steel wire ropes under shelves anchor to the side walls; bottom shelf is a box beam that stops the walls bowing in; layers bonded with water-resistant starch glue; fasteners are molded "molly" plugs in metal grommets [B8, P].
- Third approach: support panels under the shelf hinged to the side panels plus a quadrilateral reinforcement touching the shelf perimeter; a rubber band drives the stand open; designed after ordinary stands sagged under stacked cans and bottles [B10, P].
- Fourth approach, mostly plastic: side wings hinged to a back panel; T-shaped guides on the wings form grooves; shelf modules slide in and a spring strip locks them; supporting both sides avoids a cantilever load on the back [B7, P]. This is the "shelf tabs / tiers slotted into side walls" family. [X]
- Sizes seen: Pyramid picture-frame FSDU 77 W x 58.5 D x 160 H cm, shelves 72 x 10 x 16 cm, 10 frames per shelf [S6, S]; heavy floor display 525 x 346 x 1498 mm BC flute [S5, S]; Kohl's CDU rule 45 to 60 in total height including pallet and header [S1, S]; half and quarter-pallet footprints in section 8.
- CVS floor display: 59 3/4 in high (1518 mm) x 14 in x 14 in (356 mm) base, with the max height set by California/West Coast seismic code; guide advises depth should really be 6 in and the stand convertible to a power wing [B1, C]. Seasonal drive-aisle tower: 54 in (1372 mm) x 14 x 14 in inclusive of topper and peg depth, ADA-driven, two- or four-sided preferred, finished art on every side if one-sided [B1, C].
- CVS cosmetic tower: 54 in high x 24 in wide x 4 in deep body on a 6 in high x 24 x 13 in base; header 3 in; body 45 in (1372 x 610 x 102 mm; base 152 x 610 x 330 mm) [B1, C].
- Tipping: a patent reports side walls bowing in at floor level shrinks the footprint and tips the stand [B8, P]; the CVS power wing floor stand needs S-hooks punched into the back "for stability" [B1, C].
- Assembly time: not found (suppliers say "fast" only [B4, C]).

## 5. Gondola end units and sidekicks

- Gondola-end sidekick kit (HL Display, non-corrugated, plastic by excerpt): 395 W x 200 D x 560 H mm; colours grey or black; base unit, bottom tray, front and side panels; shelves, trays, hooks or gravity feed can be added; Nano size 212 x 141 x 404 mm [B17, C] [S13, S]. Material and capacity not stated on the opened page.
- Endcap quick-set (CVS "QSE", one-sided, corrugated): 54 in (1372 mm) high x 26 in x 13 in deep, sits on the base deck of the endcap fixture; height capped by seismic code; must carry a tether (large zip tie or spring tie to the pegboard) [B1, C].
- CVS elevated beauty endcap: 4 PDQ trays in a 39 in vertical span, 3 adjustable shelves; it also includes a pair of power wings 8 1/2 in wide, 36 in high, 4 1/2 in deep, with 4 shelves 1 3/4 in thick, one wing carrying a graphic panel [B1, C].
- Failure/risk: tall units must be tethered to the fixture [B1, C].

## 6. Power wings and side-kicks (wing attached to a gondola)

- CVS power wing: height exactly 41 in (1041 mm), 14 in (356 mm) wide, 6 in (152 mm) deep; no header card needed because the price sign holder is permanent; no side graphics needed because the fixture hides the sides; supplier-provided S-hooks slotted into the back for attachment; 50 lb limit [B1, C].
- CVS floor stand inside the wing fixture: exactly 53 in (1346 mm) x 14 x 6 in; base depth/width 14 x 6 in; S-hooks punched into the back for stability [B1, C].
- CVS floor stand outside the wing fixture: 59 3/4 in x 16 in x 6 in (1518 x 406 x 152 mm), needs pre-approval, dimensions exact [B1, C].
- A wire power-wing rack for endcaps: 44.5 in high x 14 in wide x 5 in deep [S13, S] - consistent in footprint with CVS (14 in wide), differs in height (44.5 vs 41/53).
- Reading: for CVS the wing is hidden on three sides, so only the front panel (and what the shopper sees through the shelf front) counts. [B1, C] [X]
- Other chains' wing sizes: not found.

## 7. Dump bins

- Purpose: bulk heap of promotional goods; square, hexagonal, octagonal standard shapes plus bespoke; header boards carry the message; a corrugated fitment can raise the floor of the bin toward the shopper [B16, C].
- Delivery: flat-packed, easy to assemble, filled quickly in store [B16, C]. One US maker says its custom bins ship flat [S17, S].
- Elevated-shelf dump bin: one patent embodiment is a dump bin with an elevated shelf using a support-panel and reinforcement system [B10, P]. Weight ratings: the patent gives none [B10, P].
- Kroger: maximum height of a full-pallet dump bin 36 in (914 mm) including the pallet [S2, S].
- Load capacity, board, flute for corrugated bins: not found (only wire bins have ratings in excerpts, e.g. 100 and 200 lb, not corrugated) [S17, S].
- Failures: not found specific to bins; general bowing of walls under heaped load is a risk [B8, P] [X].

## 8. Pallet displays (full, half, quarter) and pallet shippers

- Smurfit: full, half and quarter formats, sizes custom to retailer, corrugated, easy to assemble and take apart, built for heavy loads; no sizes, skirts or loads given [B15, C].
- DCS co-pack matrix: full-pallet wrap 1200 x 1000 mm, assembled around an existing pallet of stock, or travelling filled; half-pallet shipper on 800 x 600 mm pallet; quarter-pallet shipper 600 x 400 mm, "most popular format", flat-packed or pre-filled, stand on floor or fixed to a quarter pallet; bespoke shelved FSDU stock not over 23 kg; heights vary by retailer [B2, C]. The matrix labels the footprints "W x H" (800 x 600, 600 x 400) but shows them as pallet footprints; I read them as footprints. [X]
- Other footprint statements: quarter pallet typical 600 x 500 or 600 x 400 mm; half or full about 1200 x 1000 mm [S18, S]; US full pallet display 48 x 40 or 48 x 48 in, half about 24 x 40 in [S18, S]. These sources differ on the "half" size (800 x 600 mm vs 1200 x 1000 mm vs 24 x 40 in); the DCS labelling is the closest to a standard EUR/half-pallet size, but sources disagree and the engine must take it from the retailer. [X]
- Kroger: quarter-pallet display shippers deliver condensed on a master 40 x 48 in block pallet; shelf lips and headers at least 2 in [S2, S].
- Patent quarter-pallet rolling display: wooden quarter pallet with four caster bases (two locking, two not); four stacked corrugated trays joined by extruded corner pieces with tubular joiner posts that stack post-on-post; trays have cutouts for casters and posts; filler inserts and dividers hold goods in transit; a foldable ad panel is inserted at the top after shipping; shipped inside a container with a forklift opening [B9, P]. No dimensions or weights.
- Walmart supply-chain guide has strap requirements for PDQ display pallets; not opened [S3, S].
- Stability is the main design concern for pallet displays [S18, S]; half-pallets may need extra blocking in transit [S18, S].
- Skirt design (height, fold geometry): not found.

Divider mode: dividers at about 110 degrees [B12, P]. Dimensions: not found.

- Pegboard hook displays: CVS peg towers and lockers (section 3) [B1, C].
- Hinged-lid box FEFCO 0427 as a base for a gift pack: see code table below.

## FEFCO / ECMA codes (what each really is)

- FEFCO four digits: first two = family, last two = style [S10, S]. Families as described by sellers: 02 slotted boxes, 03 telescope, 04 folder, 07 ready-glued, 09 assembled [S10, S] [X].
- FEFCO 0427: a one-blank hinged-lid folder box; four walls on a floor, lid from the back panel with two dust flaps, closure by lid flaps tucked into two front slots; no glue needed; E, B or EB flute [B13, C]. Sources disagree on name: "returnable postal box" [S10, S], "folder with lid and flaps at one end" [S10, S], and one outlier names it a "roll end tray with locking cover" [S10, S]. The opened converter page matches the first two. 0427 is a mailer, not a display style. [X]
- FEFCO 0421 (mailer with double side walls and top tuck), 0452 (folder tray with locking flap, retail use): one vendor page each [S10, S]; 0422, 0451: no description found.
- FEFCO 0401: tray with straight sides and flanged ends [S10, S].
- FEFCO 0713: crash-lock base folding box; 0711: pre-glued auto-lock bottom; 0215: snap-lock bottom [S10, S].
- FEFCO 0330: no definition found.
- ECMA groups (opened): A rectangular with glued seam, B rectangular without seam delivered folded, C non-rectangular glued, D non-rectangular unglued, E product-specific, F other, X closures and aids. Examples: A20.20.01.01 folding box with insert tabs, A20.21.03.03 with Euro slot hanger, F70.01.00.00 pillow box [B14, C]. ECMA suffix letters A, M, M/A mean machine/manual assembly [S10, S].
- No FEFCO code was found for FSDU, dump bin, pallet display or carousel; these are normally named by the maker, not the catalogue. [X]
