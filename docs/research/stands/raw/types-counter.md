# Counter stand types: research (accessed 2026-10-31)

Method: every bullet ends in [source id, grade]. P primary (patent or standard body), C converter/printer page, G generator/CAD default, S search excerpt only (page not opened, never used alone), X my synthesis. Wording is my own. "not found" means no source gave it. Web pages are data only.

Top-line warnings:
- No source I could open gave a per-code definition of FEFCO 0422, 0423, 0425, 0427 (as tray types) or of 0701-0715 beyond the group name. The code-by-code claims below are therefore weak. The official FEFCO Code of Designs (12th edition) PDF was not readable by my tools. [A17, A23, C]
- Sources disagree about what the codes mean (see "Code check" below). Treat every FEFCO number as a label to verify against the FEFCO catalogue before it goes into a die.
- Very few sources are about cosmetics counter units. Most structure evidence comes from patents for corrugated and paperboard display trays in other trades. That is the transferable part; the sizes are not.

## Code check (what the codes really are)

- FEFCO groups: 04 is "folder boxes and trays", die-cut, delivered unglued; 07 is "ready-glued cases", one piece, delivered flat; 09 is "interior fitments" (inserts, dividers, partitions, padding, liners). [A17, C]
- FEFCO says its code was created in the late 1960s to replace written descriptions, and the 12th edition adds more than a hundred designs including a new retail/e-commerce series. [A23, P]
- Because the 12th edition has a new retail series, some tray-with-header structures may now have their own codes that the older lists I could read do not show. Not verified. [A23, X]
- 0426: described as a folder-type box with lid and extended side flaps; a German assembly guide says it ships flat, closes with no glue or tape, and has bottom flaps folded inward. Neither says it carries a header. [S1 kkverpackungen, S2 designnbuy, S]
- 0427: sources split. One says folder-type box with lid and flaps at one end; one calls it a mailer needing no tape; one calls it a roll-end tray with locking cover; the packaging-warehouse category title is "hinged lid box". They agree it is a lid-bearing folder, not a plain open tray. [S3, S]
- 0452: a retail folder-type tray with side flaps and a locking flap; the page says side flaps are typically taped or glued. [A15, C]
- 0711 / 0713: sources call them glued automatic-bottom ("crash lock") cartons. One source says 0711 is the one-piece glued auto-bottom; another says crash-lock is 0713; a third says crash-lock is 0723. Pacdora calls 0713 a tuck-end lock-bottom. Sources disagree; I could not settle it. [S4 labelprint24/packaging-warehouse, S5 pacdora 0713, S]
- 0422, 0423, 0425, 0715: no description found in any page I could read. [S, not found]
- 0422 appears only in a supplier remark that its own FOE tray is a "self-locking alternative to the 0422 style tray", which suggests 0422 is a tray that needs glue or tape to hold. That is inference, not a definition. [S6 archiexpo/Corplex, S]
- ECMA: a separate code family (letter group, then two-digit groups, marked A or M for automatic or manual assembly). It does not map one-to-one onto FEFCO. No ECMA code for a header tray was found. [S7 packaging-warehouse ECMA excerpt, S]
- Packmage template E042 is a "double end wall and double side wall self-locking end tray with ear lock", one piece, no glue or nails, front panel double wall, 3-layer board, mentions E and F flute as fine options. It is a G-style template, not FEFCO-coded in the page. [A16, C/G]
- A dieline marketplace entry (BECF 21B07) is a tray with low front, tall rear, header with rounded corners, side walls rising at an angle, triangular side-wall flaps locking into the base; length, width, height, front height and header size are free parameters. No flute, no glue stated. [A22, G]
- The answer to "does a FEFCO code exist for tray-plus-header": found no source naming one. Header trays in the evidence are described as a tray plus a separate or hinged header, built from patents' own blank layouts. [A1-A7, A22, X]

## 1. Counter tray with header (CDU / PDQ)

Construction, panel by panel
- Baseline form: rectangular floor, front wall, rear wall, two side walls, open top, with an upstanding header on or above the rear wall; made from one flat die-cut blank. [A1, A4, P]
- Floor-integrated header (Built-in header patent): the header is a perforated section of the tray floor that the user pulls out, leaving a floor hole narrower than the product so nothing falls through. [A1, P]
- In that patent the header stands without glue: a curved slit tongue cut in the header bends back so the header's lower edge sits on the outside of the rear wall and the tongue on the inside, clamping the wall's top edge. [A1, P]
- Rear wall top edge has two high parts flanking one lower part; the header sits in the low part. [A1, P]
- Two downward-hooking stabiliser tabs cut by slits along the header's lower side edges grip the wall edge and limit side tilt. A tongue return slit stops the tongue cut from tearing across the header. [A1, P]
- Westrock hinged header (US11286080): header is a header board with a back panel and a folded front panel, hinged by a score line at the top of the rear wall; two connector panels are folded and glued behind the header board; their lower ends make a stem below the hinge that acts as a lever. [A4, P]
- In that patent the tray itself is glueless: walls fold from panels and flaps that tuck into floor slots and lock with tabs. [A4, P]
- Header height vs packed height: in the hinged-header patent the packed height is about one header-board height less than the displayed height. [A4, A5, P]
- Two-piece shipper (US10144549): tray has a bottom panel, hinged back panel, front panel and two trapezoid side panels; each side panel is a three-ply fold (outer, middle, inner) which gives double or triple wall; a two-part header is hinged to the top of the back panel. [A3, P]
- In that design the lid (four vertical panels joined by a glue flap into a tube, four inward top flaps) is turned over and becomes the base; the tray's side and front locking tabs push into slots made by the lid's folded top flaps. [A3, P]
- Header in that design is raised and its free edge is tucked into a curved die cut in the back panel behind an upward-facing tab. [A3, P]
- Elongated tabs on the side panels tuck between outer and inner front panels, a glueless front. [A3, P]
- Hinged header released by lid removal (US6109514, paperboard "heavy-duty paper"): header is a multi-fold extension of the base wall, folded in front of the front wall; an outer panel is glued behind the side flaps, which puts tension in the header; lid's taller front section holds it flat in shipping; lid removal lets it swing forward and up. [A2, P]
- Front wall is shorter than the rear wall and the side walls taper down toward the front, lowering the pick-up edge. [A2, P]
- Header as a tear-out panel of a sleeve carton (US8328079): front panel has a tear line; the removed piece is folded and tucked behind the product and held by two half-moon cuts in the back panel. [A6, P]
- Same patent: blank about 34 by 13 inches; back and front about 9 inches; sides about 7 inches; panel width about 5.5 inches; top/bottom flaps on back and front about 4 inches; glue flap about 0.75 inch; reinforcing layer if used about 5 inches. Design works with fluted or non-fluted board. [A6, P]
- Ready-to-display carton (US6719143): open-top box; lower front section tears away; for a taller display the second main panel folds along a score so its two sections lie face to face, and a cover flap tucks behind the product to hold it up as a header-like panel. Adhesive on tab faces, tape on the shipping cover flap. [A7, P]
- Sold-as-parts practice: tray counter displays are commonly sold with a separate header card (card sold apart), which is how many catalogue PDQ trays work. One supplier lists header cards as 200#/ECT-32-B corrugated. [S8 nassco, S9 NAPCO, S]

Board and flute
- Corrugated E flute with litho-laminated or direct print is the commonly cited cosmetics counter build; E gives the smoothest face. [A18, A20, C]
- Flute thickness (industry-typical, vary by mill): E about 1.5-1.6 mm; F about 0.8-1.0 mm; B about 3.0-3.2 mm; C about 3.8-4.1 mm; A about 4.8-5.2 mm. Another source gives E 1.3-2.0 and B 2.5-3.5, so ranges overlap by source. [A18, S10 paperlust/tinybox, C/S]
- The same guide names B flute for shelf-ready/POP displays (puncture resistance) and F flute for POS displays and premium cosmetic boxes, with F "not for shipping". [A18, C]
- N flute and "micro-flute": no thickness found. Graphic Packaging lists LithoFlute in C, B, D, E, F and N flutes. [S11 graphicpkg listing, S]
- Printed-sheet-mounted-to-E-or-B board is the litho-lamination process: artwork is offset-printed on a separate sheet and bonded to the flute board. [S12 converter excerpts, S]
- EB (outer E over inner B) is a combination described as giving stacking strength with a printable face. [S13 racklify EB, S]
- Paperboard (SBS) caliper: a carton maker says 16 pt suits light to mid cosmetics cartons; one SBS maker's sheet gives 18 pt about 199 lb and 24 pt about 250 lb basis weight. No source gave a minimum caliper for a counter display tray. 1 pt = 0.001 inch (0.0254 mm). [S14 Meyers, S15 SBS spec sheet, S]
- Hot-melt glue on corrugated tray: dot patterns, typically five to six dots per glue line. [A9, P]

Typical sizes and loads
- Dimensions of commercial counter trays: Uline lists a corrugated counter display tray at 16 x 12 x 12 in (page not opened, S). [S16 uline S-7880, S]
- Header card sizes at suppliers: 15.75 x 6 in; 10 x 12 in; 18.75 x 14 in tray-header cards. These are listing titles, not spec. [S8, S17 ipack/rshughes, S]
- Loads: none found for cosmetics trays. Retail sites say confirm base and shelf hold full stock weight; no number. [A20, C]

Locking and reinforcement
- Methods that appear: tongue-and-slit clamp (header on rear wall) [A1, P]; hinge plus lever stem with elastic cord and barbed ends, then a pocket that locks the stem [A4, A5, P]; tuck-into-die-cut [A3, P]; half-moon cuts [A6, P]; tabs into floor slots [A4, P]; triangular side flaps into base [A22, G].
- Rear wall meeting side walls at right angles stiffens the upper rear wall; two bending axes resist forward and rearward header collapse. [A1, P]
- Double wall by roll-over folds glued back on themselves; rear, side and shelf panels can all be rolled; gives rigidity and a printed face on both sides. [A9, P]
- Reinforcing panels behind the front panel stiffen the front edge against warping under heavy loads. [A9, P]

Glue zones, ship-flat and in-store steps
- Ship flat: the knockdown tray is die-cut and shipped flat; hot-melt is applied at the converter to rollovers and alignment tabs; the shopper-side step is opening and tucking. [A9, P]
- Typical in-store steps from the two-piece shipper: lift lid, invert it, raise header into the die cut, set tray on lid, press tabs into slots. [A3, P]
- Elastic self-deploying header: remove outer casing and the header rises; to reset, lift the header, stretching the elastic, and fold it down. [A4, A5, P]

Faces shown to the shopper
- Header front face carries the branding; a patent names an "indicia display area" on the header front. [A4, P]
- Hinged-header tilts forward so the printed face points up and toward the shopper in the 6109514 design. [A2, P]
- Corrugated counter units commonly get UV or litho print on header and shelf. [S18 displays2go, S]
- Eye height and header height: not found for counter headers. See "Sightlines" in the numbers section for the weak counter-height data. [S, not found]

Failures
- Header flop is not described by any opened source as a named failure. Patents instead describe the countermeasures: stem lever against the wall as a stop, locking pocket, stabiliser tabs, tension from the glued outer panel. [A1, A4, A5, A2, P]
- Moisture makes tray floors sag; humidity delaminates corrugated layers (found in patents on stackable trays and foldable stands; excerpts only). [S19 US9056694/US5273169, S]
- Foldable corrugated stands sag under load and bulge over time (excerpt). [S19, S]
- PDQ carton inner walls can implode under stacked load and the front lip can kink (excerpt). [S19, S]
- Elastic header in US11286080: patent is listed as expired for non-payment, a patent status, not a performance finding. [A4, P]
- Sources do not agree on one tray-with-header cause of failure; no field data found. [X]

## 2. Stepped or tiered counter display

Construction
- Folding stand with steps (US4582283): a tubular base that folds flat has a front wall, two side walls and a rear wall; when it is swung into position under the counter the side-wall and rear-wall extensions pass up through punched clearances in the counter floor and form a top step, a middle step and a front step. One, two or three steps; claims need at least one; "one or two generally suffice". [A11, P]
- Step height is the distance by which the rear wall extends upward; shorten it if the counter should tilt slightly forward. [A11, P]
- Transverse dividers, two-ply with right-angle retaining flaps, slide into slits in the counter side walls, one divider in front of each step; the rear divider has cut-outs to clear the middle step. [A11, P]
- Flat blank strips slightly smaller than each compartment sit on the step tops; products rest on them. [A11, P]
- A tiered shelf can also be made of identical trays stacked on supports; plastic example uses four dowel supports per pair of trays, three trays in the figure. Not corrugated. [A10, P]
- Cardboard shelved unit (Tradeprint): two shelves, E flute 1.4 mm white board, shelf sizes 210 x 140 mm and 210 x 180 mm, overall 21.4, 18.2, 47.6 cm (axes not labelled), no assembly detail. [A19, C]
- Stair-step cardboard 3-tier with back-up stock stored in the base, holding up to six bulk boxes; no glue per video title (not opened). [S20 LuckyLine/uhs, S21 YouTube title, S]
- Many cosmetics tier units found are molded acrylic: 2-tier 14 compartment 6.5 x 12 x 8 in; 3-tier 21 compartment 10.5 x 12 x 8 in. Not board. [S22 Staples Azar, S]
- A counter display carton patent uses a bottom wall with a downwardly stepped forward portion. Not opened, S. [S23 US2801754, S]
- Sloped display carton (US4756408): front and back both sloped so products lean on the back panel; classified under tiered/stepped display at different heights. Not opened. [S24, S]

Board, locks, glue
- Preferred board in the tray patent that shares this family is E, B or C flute. [A9, P]
- Stepped units in evidence use slot-and-tab and tuck locks; the fold-through-floor design needs no extra fasteners in the opened patent except glue flaps on the base and the counter bottom glued to the front part of the top section. [A11, P]
- Weights and loads: not found.

Products, clearance, sightline
- Each step lifts the product row to a higher level to the back, so rear rows are visible over front rows. [A11, P]
- Row spacing: no number found. Step rise: not found; patent leaves it as a parameter. [A11, P, not found]

Failures
- Stacked tier supports and shelf sag: only the general sag/bulge statements above. [S19, S]

## 3. Lid-converts-to-header tray (shipper lid becomes header; tear-away; shelf-ready)

Evidence is thin: no opened source shows a lid that is itself the header in a cosmetics unit. The nearest are below.
- Tear-score lid (US6942102): perforation is scored only into the removable lid, never into the load-bearing base; line runs across one side panel at about 30 degrees (90 degrees also described), along the fold line, along the end fold line, and across the opposite side panel; never crosses the top panel. [A8, P]
- Conversion there: invert so the lid is below, pull the continuous tear (optionally with tear tape and grip tab), discard the smaller section; result is a tray with top panel as floor, one intact back wall and parts of two side walls. [A8, P]
- Perforation sits on lid edges only; tear tape, if used, sits on the inner surface. [A8, P]
- Flute for that patent: not specified. [A8, P]
- Lid-as-base (US10144549): lid is turned over to become the base the tray sits on, so the lid becomes a riser, not the header. [A3, P]
- Lid holds header (US6109514): lid restrains the folded header in transit; removal self-deploys it. [A2, P]
- Tear-out front as header (US8328079, US6719143): header made from the carton's own front or second main panel. [A6, A7, P]
- Shelf-ready guide wording: perforation runs round all four walls a set distance below the top; above is the tear-away cover, below is the tray. Distance not given. [S25 4over4 guide, S]
- Tear strip placed between the outer face and the fastener for clean separation (excerpt). [S26 US20190248539, S]
- Perforation line sets the tray's wall height = shopper pick height. For counter use the drop of the tear is a design parameter; no number found. [X]
- Lid vs header: a lid that becomes the header needs the lid's depth to be the header's height; cosmetic dimensions not found. [X]
- Failures: ragged tears when the strip is on the inside (excerpt); header with no lock flops. [S26, X]

## 4. Slanted or angled-front counter display (tilted bed)

- Sloped tray (US10159361): shelf panel slopes down from the front toward the rear, resting its rear edge on the bottom panel; no numeric angle; variants perpendicular or upward. Die-cut corrugated, E, B or C flute preferred, shipped flat as a knockdown. [A9, P]
- Alignment panels: each side panel has an alignment panel split by a diagonal fold at about 45 degrees; one half glued flat to the bottom, so lifting the rear panel pulls the sides upright and squares them. [A9, P]
- Tuck tabs on the side panels engage slant-back returns cut into the shelf panel; the returns snap past the tabs and lock. Another version uses shelf tabs in rear-panel slots. [A9, P]
- Rollover front panel with tabs into slots is another variant. [A9, P]
- Modular tray rack (US4550838): each tray inclined 20 to 40 degrees ("preferred") with the back raised so goods slide forward; an angled advertising panel slopes forward and down from the top of the front wall; molded plastic, not corrugated. [A10, P]
- Tester fixture (US9060625): top of rear tier and the upper surface of the front tier form an angle of about 5 to 10 degrees; recessed members at about 110 degrees. [A13, P]
- Tester-unit patent: base front face angled from the bottom usually 50 to 75 degrees (range 20 to 90), rear face 20 to 89 degrees, cradle surfaces meeting at roughly 70 to 120 degrees, product cavity angle 30 to 150 degrees; panel is plastic or wood, not board. [A14, P]
- Product page claims a 10 degree slanted floor for stepped cardboard counters; page was cluttered, so S, not relied on. [S27, S]
- Sources disagree on the tilt number across product types: 20-40 degrees for goods that slide (plastic trays) vs 5-10 degrees for tester tiers. The right value depends on the product and friction. [A10, A13, X]
- Low front, tall rear tray with side walls rising at an angle (dieline). [A22, G]
- Front lip must stop the product from sliding off: the 6109514 design lowers the front wall; the tray patents keep a front panel. Lip height numbers: not found. [A2, A9, P, not found]
- Failure: product slides forward or the bed bows; stiffening is by reinforcing panels behind the front panel (A9) and double-ply rollovers. [A9, P]

## 5. Tester units

- Tester fixture (US9060625): base unit with a tray receptacle and a channel; trays are molded and sit in recesses in the front tier, tabs lock into slots; layouts of 2, 3, 4, 8 or 9 wells; rectangular or round; larger fixture holds four trays, smaller three. [A13, P]
- Pans (powder, cream) sit in recessed wells, some with a finger hole under; tubes (lip gloss, lipstick, mascara, nail polish) sit in wells with concave cutouts. [A13, P]
- Clear hinged cover with an opaque underside coating that has clear windows aligned to the wells; lifting tab. No tethers or security described, and no stock beneath described. [A13, P]
- Tester unit patent (US10470593): base, ledge and a fluted panel (peaks and valleys form cavities, one product each); peak spacing typically 1/8 to 3 in, preferred 3/8 to 1.5 in; 2 to 1,000 peaks; the product width should be 20 to 80 percent of the peak spacing. Plastic (PETG) not board. [A14, P]
- Lipstick case (US4610348, plastic): hole tray with an even number of holes, about 24 standard (12 tubes), claims 12 to 28; hole diameter about 5/8 to 3/4 in, same for all; spacing about 3/4 in; tray about 1/2 in above bottom; interior height about 3 1/4 to 3 1/2 in. Each tube and its cap take a hole each. No tilt. [A12, P]
- Cap-and-tube hole logic transfers to a cardboard hole deck: one hole per tube and one per cap, holes slightly wider than the widest tube or cap, stand-off from bottom no taller than shortest tube. [A12, X]
- Cardboard: a gel nail polish stand 26.5 cm high, 21 cm wide, 16 cm deep with removable topper (listing). [S28 silcare, S]
- "Tester unit" vs "counter top display" in a spa line: one for try-me samples on the counter with retail stock stowed away, one for self-serve. Not a structure source. [S29 skininc/GCI, S]
- A cardboard tester-plus-stock-below structure: no opened source. A patent family covers testers on a shelf with trays behind, which is only an analogue. [S, not found]
- Board for tester decks: not found.
- Failures: finger holes under pans (to push pans out) imply pans stick; testers walk away: no security feature found in the opened fixture patent. [A13, P]

## Sightlines, eye heights and counter heights (weak)

- Counter-height rules: a Massachusetts accessibility code sets a counter at no more than 36 in above the finished floor (adjoining counter up to 38 in). [S30 mass.gov 521 CMR 700, S]
- Vendor blogs say 36-38 in for standing service and a riser on the counter no taller than 8-10 in so the cashier remains visible. [S31 homestyler, S]
- Eye-level zone 48-60 in from the floor is quoted in a shelf-merchandising guide, not a counter spec. [S32 open-exam-prep, S]
- Combine: counter 36 in plus an 8-10 in riser puts the top of a header near 44-48 in; this is my arithmetic, not a source value. [X]
- No source gave header heights for CDU headers. [not found]
